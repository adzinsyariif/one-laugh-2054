# Candidates — One Line, One Laugh

Judge brief (from competition.yaml): deadpan comic who has heard every joke
twice. Ranked criteria: (1) actually a funny one-line joke, (2) laugh from a
turn / surprise / one exact word, not explanation or meme, (3) one short line
readable in a breath, (4) fresh wording in plain spoken words, (5) lands on a
single read with no context needed. Length for its own sake does not count.

All candidates below were written from scratch for this entry.

## Candidates

1. "I don't have a morning routine, I have a morning negotiation."
   - Strengths: very short, one exact word ("negotiation") carries the turn,
     plain spoken words, lands instantly with zero context.
   - Weaknesses: slightly aphoristic; reads a touch like a caption. The joke
     is real but abstract — no concrete image for the judge to picture.

2. "My umbrella works great — it keeps exactly one shoulder dry."
   - Strengths: classic setup/punch in one breath. "Works great" sets up
     praise, then "exactly one shoulder" reverses it with comic precision —
     the word "exactly" does the work (criterion 2). Concrete visual image,
     deadpan understatement, fresh observational angle, no meme, no shared
     knowledge needed. Short enough to read in a breath (criterion 3).
   - Weaknesses: none material. Universal experience, plain words.

3. "I put my phone on Do Not Disturb and my anxiety said, 'bold of you to think I'm a notification.'"
   - Strengths: fresh personification, exact-word punch ("notification").
   - Weaknesses: longest of the set; needs two clauses to land, so it loses
     on criterion 3 against a shorter line. Slightly more "written" than
     spoken.

4. "My fitness tracker thinks I'm dead, which honestly feels supportive."
   - Strengths: short, dry turn at "supportive".
   - Weaknesses: the fitness-tracker-thinks-I'm-dead premise has circulated
     before; a judge who has "heard every joke twice" may score it down on
     criterion 4 (freshness).

5. "I finally cleaned my desk and found three versions of myself who also said they'd clean the desk."
   - Strengths: fresh, self-deprecating, clear turn.
   - Weaknesses: longest candidate; the laugh arrives late, weaker on
     criterion 3. Needs the full sentence to pay off.

6. "My grocery list says 'healthy' and my cart says 'we've been through this before'."
   - Strengths: relatable, plain words.
   - Weaknesses: dialogue format leans on a familiar two-voice meme shape;
     weaker on criterion 2 (leaning on a familiar form).

7. "I bought noise-canceling headphones to ignore my own thoughts more efficiently."
   - Strengths: one exact word ("efficiently") as the kicker.
   - Weaknesses: premise is closer to a stray thought than a joke with a
     turn; risks criterion 1 against a clearer joke.

8. "I don't count sheep to sleep, I count unread emails and immediately pass out from dread."
   - Strengths: clear turn on a familiar phrase.
   - Weaknesses: leans on the familiar "counting sheep" setup (criterion 2/4
     risk) and runs long.

## Pick

Candidate 2: "My umbrella works great — it keeps exactly one shoulder dry."

Why it wins pairwise under the brief, in criterion order:

- Criterion 1: unambiguously a joke, not a caption — praise, then reversal.
- Criterion 2: the laugh is one exact word, "exactly", plus the precise
  image of a single dry shoulder. No explanation, no meme.
- Criterion 3: one line, one breath, nothing wasted.
- Criterion 4: fresh observational wording in plain spoken words; not a
  joke the reader has seen a hundred times.
- Criterion 5: lands on a single read; everyone has held an umbrella.

Candidate 1 was the runner-up and was the first line written into main.ts;
it was replaced with candidate 2 because the concrete image and the
"exactly" precision give the judge a clearer, more decisive laugh, while
staying just as short.
